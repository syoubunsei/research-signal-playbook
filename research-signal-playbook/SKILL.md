# Research Signal Playbook

Run research in two layers:

1. Build a factual base.
2. Convert facts into patterns, implications, and recommended moves.

Default to fast, high-signal synthesis. Do not stop at description. Always answer:

- What is happening?
- Why is it happening?
- What does it imply?
- What should the team do next?

## Workflow

### 1. Frame the research question

Define the minimum useful scope before collecting data:

- Category, competitor set, or product trend
- Time horizon
- Decision the research should support
- Output format expected by the user

If the scope is broad, narrow it to one of these modes:

- Category scan
- Competitor comparison
- Trend or signal scan
- Deep dive on one product
- Opportunity assessment

Use the templates in [references/research-templates.md](references/research-templates.md) when the user needs a structured output.

### 2. Build the sample set

Separate the landscape into distinct buckets:

- Direct competitors
- Indirect competitors
- Emerging players
- Substitutes or workarounds

Capture only the fields needed for comparison:

- Product
- Company or operator
- Core positioning
- Target user
- Platform or channel
- Pricing or monetization
- Recent momentum
- Notable risks

Use [references/sample-matrix.md](references/sample-matrix.md) for the base matrix.

### 3. Collect signal layers

Do not rely on a single source. Build a stack of signals:

- Product facts: versions, release cadence, features, onboarding, pricing
- Distribution facts: app stores, channels, landing pages, rankings
- Social signals: short-video buzz, community discussions, recurring screenshots, comments
- User clues: complaints, praise, repeated asks, friction, delight moments
- Company clues: founding time, funding, legal entity, hiring, shutdown or reliability signals
- Regulatory or policy signals when relevant

Treat signal strength carefully:

- Strong signal: repeated across sources or directly observable
- Medium signal: plausible but single-source
- Weak signal: inferred from behavior without enough corroboration

Mark inference explicitly instead of presenting it as fact.

### 4. Separate four lenses

Keep these lenses distinct during analysis:

- Facts: what is objectively observed
- Signals: what appears to be gaining traction
- Patterns: recurring structures across products or users
- Implications: what those patterns mean for strategy

This separation prevents jumping from a viral post straight to a market conclusion.

### 5. Analyze with a signal-first lens

Favor these dimensions because they surface early opportunity:

- Attention: what users notice first
- Spread: what people share and why
- Retention hooks: what may bring users back
- Payment triggers: what users might pay for
- Trust and risk: what can break adoption

Use the evaluation prompts in [references/evaluation-lenses.md](references/evaluation-lenses.md).

### 6. Synthesize into a clear point of view

Move from notes to judgment:

- Which products are winning attention?
- Which products have real product depth versus only buzz?
- Which user need is actually being satisfied?
- Which mechanics are reusable?
- Which risks are structural?
- Where is the window of opportunity?

Do not produce feature inventory without a conclusion.

### 7. End with action recommendations

Every output should conclude with recommended moves, usually grouped by:

- Product
- Growth or content
- Monetization
- Risk mitigation

Prioritize recommendations:

- `P0`: urgent or foundational
- `P1`: important next move
- `P2`: useful expansion or watchlist

## House Style

Write in a way that is:

- Signal-dense rather than academic
- Comparative rather than isolated
- Action-oriented rather than descriptive
- Specific about dates, versions, and evidence

Favor these output patterns:

- "Observed fact -> likely meaning -> implication"
- "Shared pattern across competitors -> market takeaway"
- "User signal -> product hypothesis -> recommended move"

## Quality Bar

Before finalizing, check that the work includes:

- A clear research question
- A defined sample set
- Evidence from more than one signal layer
- Separation between fact and inference
- At least one user or behavior lens
- At least one risk lens
- Actionable recommendations, not only observations
