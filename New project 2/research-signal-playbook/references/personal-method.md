# Personal Method

This skill captures a distinct research style shaped by real project work.

## Core Characteristics

### 1. Start from real movement, not static taxonomy

The workflow begins with things that are moving now:

- rising products
- unusual feature adoption
- creator buzz
- social screenshots
- user-generated recommendations
- version jumps
- sudden risk signals

This makes the output useful for product and strategy decisions, not just documentation.

### 2. Use distribution as a research surface

Distribution is treated as evidence, not just marketing noise.

Especially useful signals:

- which channel is actually driving discovery
- whether content is educational, emotional, or identity-driven
- what recurring phrases or tags show up
- whether social buzz appears larger than actual adoption

### 3. Read products through product mechanics

Do not stop at labels like "AI companion" or "social app."

Always ask:

- what is the core interaction loop
- what creates immersion
- what creates spread
- what creates repeat use
- what creates payment willingness

### 4. Turn findings into judgment quickly

The end goal is not to collect facts forever. The end goal is to state:

- what matters
- what does not
- what is reusable
- what is dangerous
- what should happen next

## Typical Blind Spots To Guard Against

- treating virality as product-market fit
- over-indexing on social heat without retention clues
- drawing strong conclusions from single-source evidence
- skipping user truth in favor of operator-side interpretation
- stopping at "interesting" instead of "actionable"

## Best Use Cases

- fast category research
- early-stage product strategy
- competitor mapping
- content or channel strategy
- investment-style opportunity scans

## Not Ideal For

- formal TAM/SAM/SOM-heavy market reports
- deep ethnographic research
- statistically rigorous survey work

In those cases, combine this skill with broader market research or JTBD methods.
